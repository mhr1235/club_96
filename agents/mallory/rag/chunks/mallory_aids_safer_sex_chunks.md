URL: local:/Users/markramos/Downloads/training corpora 2/mallory/Making It.pdf
Tags: Mallory, AIDS, survival, everyday life, safer sex, community education, public health

The pamphlet frames AIDS survival as more than facts from doctors or tests. It emphasizes applying knowledge to ordinary life: talking honestly with sex partners, drug-use partners, and friends; planning ahead; standing firm; seeking support; and treating safer sex as self-respect. Mallory can bring this to Club 96 health programming as a model of care that is practical without becoming sterile.

## Chunk: making_it_risk_is_behavior_not_identity

URL: local:/Users/markramos/Downloads/training corpora 2/mallory/Making It.pdf
Tags: Mallory, AIDS, HIV risk, identity, behavior, lesbian, bisexual, safer sex

*Making It* repeatedly separates risk from identity labels. It tells readers that whether someone calls themself gay, bisexual, straight, lesbian, or anything else matters less than what they do, what fluids are exchanged, and what protection is used. Mallory can use this to puncture Club 96 conversations that confuse identity performance with actual safety practice.

## Chunk: making_it_eroticize_protection

URL: local:/Users/markramos/Downloads/training corpora 2/mallory/Making It.pdf
Tags: Mallory, safer sex, condoms, erotic education, pleasure, AIDS, public health design

The pamphlet tries to make protection part of erotic life instead of an interruption. It discusses practicing with condoms, folding condom use into foreplay, using language and images that make safer sex playful, and treating condoms as objects people can become comfortable with. Mallory can use this as design guidance: health resources at Club 96 should be reachable, attractive, and socially normal, not hidden like shameful office supplies.

The sex work section connects safer sex to working conditions, customer pressure, police harassment, bosses, coworkers, and economic realities. Mallory can use this to keep harm reduction materialist. It is not enough to tell people to make safer choices if the room, job, law, money, or power relation punishes them for doing so.

## Chunk: making_it_drugs_needles_trust

Source: Cindy Patton and Janis Kelly, Making It: A Woman's Guide to Sex in the Age of AIDS
URL: local:/Users/markramos/Downloads/training corpora 2/mallory/Making It.pdf
Tags: Mallory, IV drug use, AIDS, harm reduction, trust, needles, community survival

The pamphlet discusses IV drug use, shared works, cleaning needles, and the way sharing among friends or lovers can symbolize trust. It reframes care as changing both drug and sex habits, finding safer supplies, and making survival part of how people show loyalty to each other. Mallory can use this history to argue that harm reduction works best when it understands intimacy, dependency, and trust.

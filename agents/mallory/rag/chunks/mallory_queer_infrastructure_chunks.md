The queer immigrant interview material gives Mallory a language for displacement that is political and intimate at once. ## Chunk: fan_clubs_as_queer_counterpublic_infrastructure

URL: local:agents/mallory/rag/sources/fanzinesarticle.pdf
Tags: Mallory, zines, fan clubs, mail art, counterpublics, Eternal Network, queer identity, community infrastructure, correspondence art

Kirsten Olds describes 1970s mail art fan clubs and fanzines as more than fandom. They circulated postcards, letters, flyers, lists, and zines through the Eternal Network, making small-scale counterpublics where artists tested individual and collective identities, including queer identities. Mallory can use this to propose Club 96 infrastructure that travels by hand, mail, photocopy, bulletin board, guest book, and invitation rather than only through platforms.

## Chunk: municipal_internet_as_civic_right

Source: Municipal Internet in New York City, Internet Report FINAL
URL: local:agents/mallory/rag/sources/Internet Report FINAL (1).pdf
Tags: Mallory, municipal internet, digital equity, community network, NYC, broadband, public infrastructure, nonprofit ISP, city property

The municipal internet report frames home internet as basic infrastructure for school, jobs, health care, housing, benefits, and civic life. It highlights Local Law 153 of 2025, which requires an internet master plan that considers nonprofit and community-based ISPs, emerging businesses, and city-owned property. Mallory can use this to connect Club 96's tech dreams to access: a queer community network should ask who can get online, who pays, who owns the wires, and who is left buffering.
